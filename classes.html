about research classes

Psychology has spent most of its history studying what goes wrong: fear, trauma, depression, and the mind's pull toward the negative (bad tends to be stronger than good; Baumeister et al., 2001). Positive psychology emerged around the turn of the century as a corrective (Seligman & Csikszentmihalyi, 2000), and one of its best-known findings is that gratitude is linked to greater well-being (Emmons & McCullough, 2003; Wood et al., 2010).

In that spirit: I'm so grateful to have taken seminars with Prof. James Gross (my advisor), Kate Petrova (my mentor), Prof. Hazel Markus, Prof. Robert Sapolsky, Prof. Anthony Wagner, Prof. Russ Poldrack, Prof. Brian Wandell, Prof. Greg Walton, Prof. Michael Frank, and Prof. Cameron Ellis. These people have changed my life, both at Stanford and outside of it, and I consider myself incredibly fortunate to have learned from them.
